Level payment or constant principal

Most mortgages and car loans in the US use a level payment: the same amount every month, mostly interest at first and mostly principal at the end. With constant principal (called SAC in Brazil, where it is common for home loans), each month repays the same share of the loan plus the interest on what is left, so the payment starts higher and falls every month, and the total interest is lower.

How AMORT works on the calculator

Compute the payment first, then AMORT splits a range of payments into interest and principal and gives the balance after the last one. The BA II Plus asks for P1 and P2; the HP 10bII+ takes the range as first INPUT last; the HP 12C amortizes the next payments each time you press f AMORT, so a range that does not start at payment 1 needs the earlier payments first. Each calculator rounds every month's interest to the display, the way banks round to the cent.

Common questions

Why is my last payment a few cents different?

With every month rounded to the cent, the last payment absorbs what the rounding left over, so the balance ends at exactly zero. The calculator does not adjust it: after the last payment its balance shows those few cents instead.

Does the schedule include taxes and insurance?

No. It shows principal and interest only. Property tax, insurance and fees come on top, and each lender adds them differently.
