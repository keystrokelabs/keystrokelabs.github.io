HP 12C guides

The HP 12C uses RPN: you key a number, press ENTER, key the second number, then the operation. Financial problems use the top row: n, i, PV, PMT, FV.

Getting started

  • HP 12C basics: ENTER (RPN), clearing, decimal places and percent

    On the HP 12C you key the numbers first and the operation last: 3 ENTER 4 + 5 × gives 35.00. Clearing, decimals, point or comma, and %, Δ% and %T.

  • HP 12C error codes: what Error 0 to 9 and Pr Error mean, and the fix

    Error 5 on the HP 12C almost always means PV, PMT and FV have the wrong signs. Every HP 12C error, from Error 0 to Pr Error, with the cause and the fix.

Loans and interest

  • HP 12C mortgage payment, interest and balance (amortization)

    A $300,000 mortgage at 6.5% for 30 years costs 1,896.20 a month on the HP 12C. Keys: f CLEAR FIN, 300000 PV, 30 g 12×, 6.5 g 12÷, PMT, then AMORT.

  • Find the interest rate of a loan or installment plan on the HP 12C

    Borrow 9,000 and repay 40 × 250: the HP 12C gives 0.52% a month (f CLEAR FIN, 9000 PV, 250 CHS PMT, 40 n, i). Also the hidden rate of "0% financing".

  • Compound interest on the HP 12C: future value, present value and rate conversion

    1,000 at 1% a month for 12 months grows to 1,126.83 on the HP 12C: f CLEAR FIN, 1000 CHS PV, 12 n, 1 i, FV. Present value and monthly vs yearly rates.

  • BEGIN vs END on the HP 12C: when to use each, and how to turn BEGIN off

    BEGIN on the HP 12C means payments at the start of each period; g 8 (END) turns it off. E.g. 36 deposits of 200 give 7,867.22 (END) or 7,906.56 (BEGIN).

Cash flows

  • NPV and IRR on the HP 12C with uneven cash flows (CFo, CFj, Nj)

    Enter cash flows with g CFo, g CFj and g Nj, the rate in i, then f NPV and f IRR. Example: NPV 16,748.73 at 10%, IRR 21.84%, and why yours may differ.

Check these before any problem

  • BEGIN off. If BEGIN shows on the display, press g8END (END) unless payments are due at the start of each period.
  • Financial registers cleared. fCLEAR FINx≷y (CLEAR FIN) erases n, i, PV, PMT and FV; values left from an earlier problem change the answer.
  • Enough decimals. f2 shows 2 decimals, f4 shows 4. The calculator always keeps the full number; only the display changes.
  • Signs. Money you receive is positive, money you pay is negative (CHS changes the sign of the number on the display).
